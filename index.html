How does FedRAMP fit into a cloud decision?

Learn what the program does, follow a federal agency through a cloud adoption decision, and practice finding the evidence that helps teams make informed choices.

Current terminology is changing. FedRAMP’s 2026 rules introduce the term “Certification” and Classes A–D. Older materials may say “authorization” and use Low, Moderate, or High impact levels. Check the current official rules and Marketplace when making a real decision.
In plain language

FedRAMP is a U.S. government program for assessing and certifying cloud services used by federal agencies. It helps agencies reuse security assessment evidence when considering cloud services that handle unclassified federal information.

What the program helps teams do

Think of FedRAMP as a shared way to examine cloud security evidence—not a substitute for an agency’s own decision.

01

Assess a cloud service

Document the service boundary, security controls, risks, and assessment evidence for the cloud offering.

02

Reuse evidence

Agencies can review existing assessment materials rather than starting every review from zero.

03

Keep monitoring

Certification is not a one-time finish line. Ongoing monitoring and risk management matter after a service is approved.

A simplified view of the work

Exact steps depend on the certification path and the agency’s situation.

PHASE 01

Prepare

Define the service, intended federal use, boundaries, responsibilities, and likely path. Engage the agency and qualified assessors as needed.

PHASE 02

Assess & decide

Review security evidence, findings, and risks. The responsible federal agency makes its risk-based decision for its use case.

PHASE 03

Monitor

Track changes, vulnerabilities, incidents, and assessment evidence over time, following applicable program and agency requirements.

FedRAMP scope depends on the federal agency’s use case. Only the federal agency can determine whether its specific use of a cloud service is within scope.

GrantFlow, a fictional cloud service

A federal agency wants to use GrantFlow, a fictional SaaS platform, to receive and review grant applications. The service stores application records, including contact details and project information. A vendor says its service has a FedRAMP listing.

CustomerU.S. federal agency
UseCollect and review grant applications
Vendor claim“We have a FedRAMP listing”
Pause before deciding: A vendor’s general claim does not confirm that the exact service, scope, status, and agency use are a fit. Verify the specific offering and involve the agency’s security, privacy, procurement, and authorizing personnel.

Activity 1: Is this use definitely in FedRAMP scope?

Good next step: verify the exact offering

Search the FedRAMP Marketplace for the product and provider. Check the listing’s current status, certification class or legacy information, service description, included services, dependencies, and authorization details. Then ask the agency whether the listed scope matches its planned use and requirements.
